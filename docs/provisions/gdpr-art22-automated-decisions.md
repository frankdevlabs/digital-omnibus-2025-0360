# GDPR Art 22 — Automated individual decision-making

**Tracked because:** the Commission sought to make solely-automated decisions easier to justify under
the contractual-necessity gateway.

## Current law
Art 22(1): right not to be subject to a solely-automated decision with legal/similarly significant effect,
save (a) contractual necessity, (b) Union/MS law, (c) explicit consent.

## Commission proposal
Rewrites Art 22 so that the contractual-necessity exception applies "regardless of whether the decision
could be taken otherwise than by solely automated means", among other clarifications (Recital 38).

## Council — ST 9547/26
- **Largely restored to current GDPR.** The right-based framing ("shall have the right not to be subject")
  is kept; the **"regardless of whether…" clause in (a) is deleted**; Recital 38 deleted.
- Paragraphs 2 and 3 (safeguards; special-category limits) retained.
- → [Operative text](../../extracts/council/ST-9547-2026_gdpr-art3-amendments.md#point-7--article-22-automated-decisions)

## Parliament (provisional)
No committee amendment touches Art 22: neither the JURI (PE789.142) nor the IMCO (PE789.877) draft opinion
tabled one, and the lead joint ITRE/LIBE draft report is pending (Oeil: "Awaiting committee decision"). On the
merits Parliament is expected to side with the Council's de-escalation (the Council largely restored the
current Art 22), but this is an expectation pending the joint report, not a tabled position.

## EDPB–EDPS
**Retain the in-principle prohibition** (Joint Opinion 2/2026, ¶66): the CJEU (*SCHUFA*, C-634/21) reads
Art 22(1) as a prohibition, not a mere invokable right, so keep "a decision … shall not be based solely on
automated processing … unless …" and confirm in Recital 38 that Art 22 stays an invokable right (¶67). Keep
"regardless of whether the decision could be taken otherwise…" **only in the recital, not the enacting
terms**, and confirm ADM is "necessary" only where **no equally effective, less-intrusive means** exist
(Arts 5(1)(c), 6(1)(b)) (¶70–72).
→ [JO 2/2026 digest: automated decisions](../advisory/edpb-edps-jo-2-2026.md#automated-decisions)

## EESC
**EESC (INT/1108)** — automated decision-making must respect **data minimisation** (Art 5(1)) and be used
only where it adds clear value and is necessary and proportionate; **human decision-making is preferred**
where reasonable. → [digest](../advisory/eesc-int1108.md)

## Related CJEU proceedings — C-568/25 (Universal Versand)
On **15 October 2026** Advocate General **Laila Medina** delivered her Opinion in **Case C-568/25**
(*Universal Versand*; reference from the Austrian **Oberster Gerichtshof**), on whether an **automated
credit check** that refuses invoice/instalment ("pay-later") payment is a solely-automated decision caught
by **GDPR Art 22**. She reads **Art 22(2)(a)** restrictively: a solely-automated decision is "necessary" for
a contract only where **no equally effective, less-intrusive alternative** (e.g. human review) exists, and
the **controller bears the burden** of showing that necessity — time/cost savings alone do not suffice
(points 63–67). This tracks the **EDPB–EDPS** reading (above) and cuts **against** the Commission's
permissive rewrite.

Directly relevant to this file: at **point 62** the AG notes that the Commission "has submitted a proposal
for a regulation … which … aims to amend the GDPR … and which would eliminate any doubt as to the
interpretation of the term 'decision' within the meaning of Article 22(2)(a) GDPR, since the regulation
would then expressly not require that a decision be based solely on automated processing" — i.e. the
**Digital Omnibus** (this file, COM(2025) 837). She **declines to give it interpretive weight** because it
"has not yet entered into force and has not even been approved by the Parliament and the Council in its
current form". The clause she describes is exactly the Commission's "regardless of whether the decision
could be taken otherwise than by solely automated means" — which the **Council has since deleted**
([ST 9547/26](../../extracts/council/ST-9547-2026_gdpr-art3-amendments.md#point-7--article-22-automated-decisions)).
An AG Opinion is **non-binding**; the Court's judgment is pending. Surfaced via a Tier-3 Bluesky post
(`T3-01`, Montezuma, [15 Oct 2026](https://bsky.app/profile/montezumachavez.bsky.social/post/3mxxaoc2nm22n)) —
see [triage #175](../triage/2026-10-16-issue-175.md).

## Status
**De-escalated by Council.** Among the less contentious items now; unlikely to be a major trilogue battle.

## Member-State signals (WK 3736/26)
- **FR** — supports **keeping the current Article 22** (prohibition + derogation), i.e. backs deleting the
  Commission rewrite.
- **PL** — **opposes deleting recital 38 / the Art 22 changes**: it objected to the "regardless of
  whether…" wording, but argues complete deletion forfeits a chance to improve legal certainty. Wants the
  **"necessity" test in 22(2)(a) read restrictively** so fully automated decisions stay exceptional —
  refine, don't remove.
- **EE** — would **prefer to continue the discussion** on automated decision-making, but is **open to the
  Commission amendment** (apply the rule "regardless of whether the decision could be taken otherwise than
  by solely automated means") as serving simplification; suggests clarifying recital 38 on contractual
  necessity (WK 3736/26 ADD 3).
