## What was flagged

Six watched sources (noyb, EDRi, netzpolitik, two Bluesky accounts — Montezuma & Schrems) report, ~18–24 Sept 2026,
a **new Irish Presidency Council compromise text on the Digital Omnibus (2025/0360)** with GDPR/ePrivacy substance:
AI processing presumed lawful under GDPR legitimate interest ("AI trumps the GDPR", "digital expropriation",
"reverse Robin Hood"), plus changes to cookies and the definition of personal data. A "step backward" vs the
June (ST 10729/26) version.

**Confirmed against the live sources (and secondary trade press):**

- The underlying primary document is **Council doc ST 12535/26**, an **Irish Presidency revised compromise text**
  dated **3 September 2026**, LIMITE, ~158 pp, interinstitutional file **2025/0360 (COD)**. noyb **published the
  leaked document on 21 September 2026**; it is **not yet on the public register** (T1-09 Austria still shows an
  earlier newest text). *(Sources: noyb 21 Sep; ppc.land; GDPRLocal; Agence Europe; netzpolitik 21 Sep.)*
- **Scope: in-scope, confirmed 2025/0360** (data/cyber — GDPR legitimate interest, cookies, pseudonymisation),
  **not** the AI-Act file 2025/0359. Both noyb and EDRi frame it as the "Digital Omnibus on Data".
- Reported/leaked substance (attributed; pending the operative text + transcription):
  - **AI legitimate interest — reintroduced.** The AI clause is **renumbered Article 88 bis** (formerly the
    Commission's standalone Art 88c) making AI development/operation a legitimate interest under Art 6(1)(f),
    backed by a recital ("processing … for the development and operation of AI *may be regarded as* … a legitimate
    interest"). Reportedly **stripped** of the Commission's unconditional objection/opt-out right, data-
    minimisation safeguards, enhanced transparency and child-specific protections. This **reverses direction**
    from ST 10729/26, which had **deleted** Art 88c (it survived only in interpretive recital 33a).
  - **Cookies — Art 88a *and* Art 88b both deleted.** Device-access consent returns to the ePrivacy Directive;
    the browser/OS machine-readable-signal recital is deleted; a **new contextual-advertising consent exemption**
    is added (measurement "based solely on the immediate content displayed during an individual visit to a single
    web page", no profiling/retention). (Art 88b deletion continues the ST 10729/26 line; the added ad-measurement
    exemption is new.)
  - **Personal data / pseudonymisation — new Article 25a**, reportedly shifting to an "insignificant likelihood"
    of identification standard tracking a 2025 CJEU ruling; EDRi warns it blurs the anonymous/pseudonymised line
    (variable legal status of identical datasets).
  - Continued restriction of data-subject rights where a request is deemed "abusive".
- NGO actions surfacing it: **noyb**, 21 Sep (*"digital expropriation"* article, publishing the leak) and 10 Sep
  (#KillTheCookieBanner civil-society open letter); **EDRi**, 24 Sep (*"Simplification for whom?"* open letter to
  Member States); **netzpolitik**, 21 Sep (*"Digitale Enteignung"*, quoting the Berlin DPA on "fundamental pillars").
