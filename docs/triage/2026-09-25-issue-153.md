It is the Tweede Kamer **vaste commissie voor Digitale Zaken (DiZa)** EU-rapporteurs' report, authored by **El Boujdaini (D66)** and **Van den Berg (JA21)**, advising the committee ahead of the **29 September 2026 inbreng schriftelijk overleg** (already tracked — [triage #100](2026-07-02-issue-100.md)) for the **informal Telecomraad of October 2026**.

Its substantive content goes well beyond a procedural recap. It reveals / relays three things not yet in the repo:

1. **A NEW Irish Presidency compromise text — ST 12535/26, issued 3 September 2026**, building on the last Cyprus text **ST 10677/26** (the interim 18 Jun text, not held), discussed in the **Antici Group (Simplification/AGS) on 11 September 2026**, and **leaked via Agence Europe**. (The repo's current "latest text" is ST 10729/26, 22 Jun 2026.)
2. **The rapporteurs' summary of the Irish text's main deltas** vs the Cyprus compromise — concentrated on the politically sensitive GDPR/ePrivacy parts:
   - *Pseudonymisation / personal-data qualification* — clarifies that pseudonymised data passed to a party that **can** identify the data subject counts as personal data **for that party**, and further processing falls under the GDPR (the EDPB pseudonymisation/anonymisation opinion is retained, as in the earlier text).
   - *AI data processing* — keeps the earlier approach: the standalone GDPR AI article stays deleted; a legitimate-interest framing survives in the recitals.
   - *Cookies* — **widens** the no-consent storage/read cases to include **contextual advertising** under safeguards (against tracking / later reuse); the Commission's automated consent-signal duty (Art 88b), already dropped by Cyprus, does **not** return.
3. **EP procedural news (relaying Brussels media):** LIBE/ITRE asked the EP research service (**EPRS**) for a **broad impact assessment** (expected **Nov/Dec 2026**), and the **LIBE/ITRE committee final vote is now scheduled for February 2027** (repo currently expects ~Q4 2026).

The report's recommendation: the DiZa 29 Sep inbreng should press the cabinet for an **appreciatie** of the Irish text (esp. personal data/pseudonymisation, AI processing, cookies/ePrivacy, incident reporting, P2B, data law), whether it changes the GDPR/ePrivacy protection level, and whether the cabinet will **await/consider the EP impact assessment** before agreeing a Raadsakkoord on the fundamental-rights parts.
