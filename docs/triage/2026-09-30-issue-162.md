## Assessment

**In scope for 2025/0360.** Passes the Dutch-language scope rule cleanly: the report's own subject is the
data/cyber Omnibus Digitaal, not the AI-Act file 2025/0359 (whose rapporteurship it declares closed). It is
a **primary Tweede Kamer scrutiny document** (a committee rapporteurs' report), downloadable and fetchable —
so it is **registered + committed**, not merely cited, consistent with how the repo treats primary NL
parliamentary/government documents.

**Substantive value — it is not procedural noise.** The report carries three things the repo's analysis
layers did not yet record (STATUS is baselined "as of 29 July 2026"):

1. **A new Irish Presidency compromise text — ST 12535/26 (3 Sep 2026).** The report states the Irish
   Presidency issued a new compromise on **3 Sep 2026 (ST 12535/26)**, building on the **last Cypriot
   compromise (ST 10677/26)**, discussed at the **Antici Group (Simplification), AGS, on 11 Sep 2026**, and
   leaked via **Agence Europe**. Per the rapporteurs the bulk of the Cypriot compromise survives; the new
   changes concentrate on politically sensitive AVG/ePrivacy points — **pseudonymisation / when data qualify
   as personal**, **AI-related data processing**, and **cookie-consent exceptions** (see their summary
   below). *Faithfulness note:* the ST 12535/26 operative text is **not in hand** (LIMITE, register-gated /
   known only via a second-hand parliamentary summary of a leak) — so no per-provision repo claim is made
   from it; it is flagged as a pending `council-text` retrieval. Note also a **numbering reconciliation** to
   verify: the repo's latest Council text is **ST 10729/26** (22 Jun mandate, never put to Coreper), with
   **ST 10677/26** as the 18 Jun interim; the rapporteurs call ST 10677/26 "the last Cypriot compromise"
   (the 10729 mandate wrapper was withdrawn).

2. **European Parliament — a firm procedural update.** Per the report the Digital Omnibus is handled
   **jointly by LIBE and ITRE**, which have **commissioned the EP research service (EPRS) to run a broad
   impact assessment** of the Digital Omnibus, **expected Nov/Dec 2026**; and the committees' **final vote is
   now scheduled for February 2027** (after which a trilogue mandate could follow). This is later than the
   repo's prior "~Q4 2026" committee-vote estimate and is recorded as reported (rapporteurs citing Brussels
   media + their own inquiry), pending OEIL/EP corroboration.

3. **Committee recommendations (the actionable core for the DiZa committee).** The rapporteurs advise the
   committee, in its **29 Sep schriftelijk overleg** ahead of the informal Telecom Council (Oct), to press
   the cabinet for: an **appreciatie of the new Irish compromise** (esp. personal-data/pseudonymisation, AI
   data processing, cookies/ePrivacy, incident reporting, P2B and data legislation); an assessment of
   **whether the new text changes the AVG/ePrivacy level of protection** and what safeguards are needed; and
   whether the cabinet will **await / take into account the EP's EPRS impact assessment** before agreeing a
   Council position on the parts touching AVG, ePrivacy and fundamental rights.

The rapporteurs' own summary of the Irish text (recorded as *their characterisation*, not repo operative
text):
- **Pseudonymisation / personal data** — the Irish text clarifies that where pseudonymised data are passed
  to a party who **can** identify the data subject, for that party the data are personal data and further
  processing falls under the AVG. (Builds on the June compromise's "out-of-scope for a party that cannot
  identify" line; the post-entry EDPB opinion on pseudonymisation/anonymisation is retained.)
- **AI data processing** — follows the earlier compromise: the standalone GDPR-for-AI article stays deleted,
  with recitals clarifying AI development/deployment can, under conditions, be a legitimate interest.
- **Cookie exceptions** — the Irish text **broadens** the cases where information may be stored/read on a
  user's device without prior consent, **now including contextual advertising** under safeguards meant to
  prevent tracking or data retention for later use; the Commission's automated consent-signal proposal
  (already dropped by Cyprus) does **not** return.
