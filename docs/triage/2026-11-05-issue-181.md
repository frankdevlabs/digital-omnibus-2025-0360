## What was flagged

EDRi analysis (**4 Nov 2026**, authored by EDRi, no individual byline) of the **Council's agreed general
position** on the data/cyber Digital Omnibus. Headline: *"EU governments call it 'simplification,' but their
Data Omnibus deal considerably weakens our rights."* Net signal: NGO confirmation that **the Council agreed
its position on 21 Oct 2026** (links the Council press release *"Digital rulebook: Council agrees position to
simplify data protection, privacy and cyber reporting rules"*, 21 Oct) and the file is **moving to the
European Parliament for first reading** ("more than 1,800 amendments tabled"). EDRi calls on MEPs to reject the
weakening of GDPR safeguards for AI and pseudonymised data and to defend ePrivacy.

Confirmed by fetching the live source (HTTP 200). EDRi's substantive reading of the agreed text:
- **Welcomes:** Council **rejected the Art 4(1) personal-data redefinition** and **dropped the Art 41a**
  pseudonymisation implementing power ("likely an unconstitutional move"); **kept device-access rules in the
  ePrivacy Directive** rather than moving them to the GDPR.
- **Opposes:** new **Art 25a** treating pseudonymised data as non-personal for third parties unable to
  re-identify; a retained **Art 88bis** AI legitimate-interest provision (Commission's Art 88c); the
  **Art 9(2)(k)** sensitive-data carve-out; new **ePrivacy exemptions** for audience measurement and
  contextual-advertising measurement, a **four-month** consent re-prompt, and **deletion of the
  machine-readable privacy-signal** duty; narrowed **access / Art 13 transparency / Art 22 automated-decision**
  protections; a new **biometric-verification** exception; and a broadened **Art 4(38)** research definition.
  Asks MEPs to reject Art 25a, delete Art 88bis and the Art 9(2)(k) carve-out, preserve access/transparency/
  automated-decision safeguards, resist further ePrivacy consent exemptions, and restore a legally binding
  machine-readable privacy signal.
