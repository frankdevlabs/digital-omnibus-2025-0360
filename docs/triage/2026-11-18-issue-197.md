## What was flagged

netzpolitik piece (Muna Turki, 17 Nov 2026, published EN + DE) reporting a new **European Trade Union
Institute (ETUI)** study by **Aida Ponce Del Castillo** and **Thomas le Bonniec** arguing the Digital
Omnibus (2025/0360) **weakens the data-protection rights of workers**. The study tests the proposed GDPR
amendments against four workplace cases that previously produced enforcement / successful claims and
concludes the same outcomes would be harder to reach.

Confirmed from the source:
- **Subject access / purpose test.** The proposal could let an employer or platform refuse a worker's
  access request on the ground it was made for a purpose *other than* exercising data-protection rights
  (e.g. gathering evidence for an employment dispute), shifting the burden onto the worker. Worked
  example: the Uber/Ola drivers whose GDPR access claims the Amsterdam Court of Appeal upheld in 2023.
- **Automated decision-making.** Today an available, equally effective human alternative is relevant to
  whether automated processing was "necessary"; the authors say the amendment changes that test so a
  possible human alternative no longer by itself shows the automation was unnecessary — strengthening an
  operator's "necessary for the contract" defence (Uber automated-fraud deactivation case).
- **No impact assessment.** The article repeats that the Commission ran *no* full impact assessment —
  the stated reason the researchers ran the study.
- Quote (Ponce Del Castillo): *"A worker may be right about unlawful surveillance and still be unable to
  prove it."*

The article names **no** GDPR / Omnibus article numbers; mapping to provisions is editorial, hedged as
the authors' reading.
