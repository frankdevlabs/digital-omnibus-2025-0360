## How to read this file

Each amendment is transcribed **as tabled**, in the document's own order and numbering (125–328), with the
two-column *Text proposed by the Commission* / *Amendment* pair reproduced and the tabling MEP(s) named.
This is a compilation of **competing** MEP proposals — several amendments target the **same** recital or
article in opposite directions, so they cannot all be adopted. Where a MEP strikes a Commission
amending-point, the *Amendment* column reads *deleted* and the **Action** is "Deleted" (the omnibus change
is removed and the underlying instrument kept); a "New" action adds a recital/article/point not in the
Commission text. Per-amendment *Justifications* are transcribed where the tabler supplied one, in their
original language. Column text is reproduced faithfully from the PDF text layer; the source's own
superscript footnote markers are rendered inline and the source's own occasional glyph artefacts (e.g.
"Regulation € 2022/1925") are left as they appear in the document.

### Amendment 137 — Recital 9 a (new)
<a id="amendment-137"></a>

- **Author:** David Cormand, on behalf of the Verts/ALE Group
- **Target:** Recital 9 a (new)
- **Action:** New

**Text proposed by the Commission**

*(none — new provision)*

**Amendment**

(9a) Connected products may be designed so that product data and related service data are accessible from on-device storage, from a local server or gateway on the user's own network, or from a remote server operated by the manufacturer or a third party. The choice among these architectures should not be used to deprive the user, in practice, of the direct and real-time access guaranteed by Article 3. Consistent with the data minimisation principle referred to in Recital 8, and given that increasingly available technology permits data to be processed locally without transmission to external infrastructure, a manufacturer should not make the exercise of the user's rights under this Regulation conditional upon the routing of data through, or storage of data on, infrastructure controlled by the manufacturer or a third party, where the same functionality can be achieved through an interface on the user's local network. The user should further be free to decide whether product data and related service data are transmitted beyond that local network, without prejudice to processing strictly necessary for cybersecurity, safety recalls, or compliance with a legal obligation to which the manufacturer is subject.

**Justification**

Explanation for new definition, to be found under amendments to Regulation (EU) 2023/2854, Article 2a and 2b.

As this access and storage constitute by itself an interference with the private sphere of a consumers as protected by Article 7 and 8 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, it begs the question whether the information qualifies as personal data. The storing of personal data, or the gaining of access to personal data already stored, in a terminal equipment should therefore continue to be allowed only on the basis of consent and be subject to clear rules able to guarantee a high level of protection of consumers while safeguarding the functioning of the internal market. Rules governing the storing of information in terminal equipment and access to such information protect the confidentiality of communications and the integrity of user devices. These protections apply independently of whether the information accessed constitutes personal data and apply regardless of whether the information accessed constitutes personal data. With a view of providing legal clarity it is necessary to define a limitative list of purposes, strictly defined and necessary, for which the processing should be permitted without consent. These exemptions should be interpreted strictly. Non-personalised advertising is a form of advertising that provides the highest level of consumer protection as it minimizes the collection of consumer data while still ensuring the provision of advertising on the internal market and the establishment of new businesses models in this sector. In the context of Article 88a (3) (d) (a) (new) it shall not involve the retention, storage, reuse or linking of any data generated in connection with such interaction, nor the processing, inference or use of any data relating to the consumer’s location whether precise or approximate, including where such location data is derived, inferred or processed in aggregated anonymized form. Contextual advertising as defined in Article 4 (b) (xx) and in the context of Article 88a (3) (d) (a) (new) shall not involve the retention, storage, reuse or linking of any data generated in connection with such interaction, nor the processing, inference or use of any data relating to the consumer’s location whether precise or approximate, including where such location data is derived, inferred or processed in aggregated anonymized form. If a controller invokes the exception of Article 88a (3) (d) (a)

(new) , the controller will not be able to engage in any other advertising activities other than contextual advertising as defined in Article (4) (b) (xx), including, but not limited, personalised and targeted advertising activities as defined in the EDPB Guidelines 8/2020 on the targeting of social media use.

Trade secret shall not be used as a pretext to deny legitimate access to data subjects and regulatory bodies. Any refusal of access must constitute a measure of last resort. Before refusing access, the data controller must offer a viable and secure alternative. This alternative must guarantee legitimate data use while ensuring the non-disclosure of trade secrets. Such alternatives may include confidentiality agreements, restricted access protocols subject to regular audits, technical measures for data aggregation or anonymization, technical architectures and standards that preserve confidentiality. In exceptional circumstances, where the data holder who is a trade secret holder is able to demonstrate that, despite the technical and organisational measures taken by the user pursuant to paragraph 6 of this Article, it is highly likely to suffer serious economic damage from the disclosure of trade secrets or that the disclosure of trade secrets to the user poses a high risk of unlawful acquisition, use, or disclosure to notably third country entities, or entities established in the Union under the direct or indirect control of such entities, which are subject to jurisdictions offering weaker or non-equivalent protection compared to that under Union law, that data holder may withhold or, as the case may be, suspend the sharing of data identified as trade secrets on a case- by-case basis a request for access to the specific data in question. That demonstration shall be duly substantiated on the basis of objective elements, such as the enforceability of trade secrets protection in third countries, the nature and level of confidentiality of the data requested, and the uniqueness and novelty of the connected product. It shall be provided in writing to the user without undue delay. Where the data holder refuses to share data pursuant to this paragraph, it shall notify the competent authority designated pursuant to Article 37. Decisions regarding refusal shall be subject to review by an independent authority that will act transparently towards the requesting user or recipient and provide effective redress.
